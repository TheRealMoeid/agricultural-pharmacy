| D31 | How product photos are stored, built for visitors on slow mobile connections. Accepted: real JPEG, PNG or WebP (judged by content), up to 10 MB and 40 megapixels. On save: orientation applied, all metadata dropped (camera model, GPS), and two WebP files are stored under a random name: a main image (longest side at most 800 px) and a thumbnail (at most 320 px) for lists. Each has a size budget, about 50 KB and 12 KB: quality starts at 80 and goes down in steps of 10 to 40, and if that is not enough the picture is made 15% smaller, up to a few times (never under 200 px). A picture that still cannot reach its budget is stored as small as those steps allow. The original is not kept. The same field type (`WebPImageField`) will be reused for other photos. | Proposed 2026-10-25 in phase 1, task 9, reworked the same day after Moeid pointed out the slow connections. Approved by merging task 9. **The numbers are estimates.** I measured WebP sizes on sample photos (not on your products): a typical 800x600 picture is about 40-80 KB at quality 80, and a busy, textured one (grass, gravel, leaves) 130-170 KB, which is why a size budget and not just a quality setting. Real product photos (packaging on a plain background) may well be lighter. After uploading a few real photos, look at the file sizes in `media/products/`: the main files should be under about 50 KB and the `_thumb` files under about 12 KB. Every number is one value in the field definition and easy to change (photos already stored are not redone). Costs: the original is gone for good; replaced or deleted photos are not removed from disk, so `media/` slowly collects unused files (they are in the backup); a very detailed photo may come out visibly softer or smaller than 800 px. The server's Nginx must allow uploads of at least 10 MB (`client_max_body_size`), its default of 1 MB would refuse most phone photos. |

## Known limitations

**D30, Jalali dates.** The conversion is the industry-standard arithmetic algorithm (jalaali-js, moment-jalaali and most Persian software use it), which approximates a calendar that is officially defined by the actual equinox in Tehran. For almost every year the two agree exactly, and the tests check that against several independently sourced Nowruz dates. There is, however, a documented and still open bug in this exact algorithm (jalaali/jalaali-js issue #52) at the boundary between Jalali 1403 and 1404, around 20-21 March 2025: a date in that window shows one day later than Iran's official calendar. Fixing it would mean replacing the arithmetic with a real astronomical calculation, a much bigger piece of work for one day near one Nowruz. Accepted as is. A test records the current behaviour, so changing it later is a deliberate decision.
